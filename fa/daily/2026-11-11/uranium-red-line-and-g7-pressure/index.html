Tehran Draws a Red Line on Uranium as the G7 Warns of Continued Pressure

Coverage date: 2026-11-11 · Publication date: 2026-11-13. Historical reconstruction: published on 13 November 2026 using reporting available for the coverage date; it was not originally issued on that date.

Daily brief: Mohsen Rezaei said Iran would not allow enriched uranium to leave the country and denied that talks were under way, while G7 statements kept pressure on the diplomatic track.

A hard nuclear position

Reuters reported that Mohsen Rezaei said Iran would not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country and warned of consequences if the United States launched new strikes. He also said no negotiations were currently taking place. This narrowed the public space for a deal based on exporting the stockpile, without ruling out every inspection or dilution arrangement.

The seven conditions remain Tehran’s framework

Al Jazeera reported that Rezaei said Tehran was waiting for a US response to its seven conditions and rejected claims that formal negotiations were under way. Trump had spoken of positive momentum after meeting Qatar’s prime minister. Washington signalled a possible path while Tehran publicly denied that talks had begun.

IranSignal assessment

Any agreement requiring removal of the stockpile would face explicit public opposition from Tehran. Possible compromise would require inspection, monitored storage, dilution or another verifiable arrangement. No such arrangement had been publicly accepted on this date.
