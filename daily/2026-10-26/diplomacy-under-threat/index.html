Iran–US: A Diplomatic Channel Is Not Yet a Deal

AP reported on 25 October that Iran said intermediary messaging continued. Trump ruled out a strike that day. Neither statement establishes a settlement.

IranSignal assessment

The distinction that matters is between access to a channel and agreement on its contents. A channel can carry warnings, refusals and proposals alike. Its survival limits one source of uncertainty, but tells us little about the distance between negotiating positions. A statement confined to one day also cannot provide a timetable for the following week. These observations support keeping both pressured bargaining and renewed escalation in the scenario set. They do not justify assigning either a numerical probability. To discriminate between those alternatives, look for reciprocal commitments, a named negotiating format and a verifiable implementation step. Until those appear, a pause can represent either room for negotiation or time used to improve leverage. This is IranSignal’s interpretation, not a reported operational assessment.

Watch for an agreed agenda, named participants, a dated meeting and matching announcements from both sides. A new operational development could supersede this assessment.
